The Problem

College students graduate unprepared for real healthcare decisions

Healthcare costs in the U.S. reached $5.3 trillion in 2024 — yet most students enter the workforce without understanding deductibles, premiums, or HSAs. The cost of confusion is high.

🎓
87.5%

Never Heard of HSAs

Of 32 UCI undergraduate students surveyed, the overwhelming majority had never encountered the term HSA before our study.

😰
60.8%

Overwhelmed by Healthcare

More than half of students reported feeling overwhelmed when choosing healthcare plans and managing health-related costs.

📉
50%

Low Confidence in Insurance

Half of students rated themselves in the lowest two levels of confidence when it comes to understanding insurance concepts.

Research

Understanding our users first

We conducted three research activities with UCI undergraduate students to identify learning gaps, validate our design direction, and iteratively improve the experience.

📋

Pre-HSA Survey

n = 32 undergraduates

Evaluated baseline knowledge of health insurance and HSAs, identified common misconceptions and learning gaps before exposure to our product.

🎤

Student Interviews

10-min sessions

Explored students' first impressions, preferences, and challenges with our prototype — helping us balance learning depth with gameplay engagement.

🧪

Usability Testing

Multi-major participants

Tested navigation, content clarity, and overall learning experience. Iterative feedback directly shaped module structure and quiz feedback design.

Key Findings

  • Students feel frustrated navigating real insurance websites — too much information, not enough context
  • Most students have heard of HSAs but genuinely don't understand how they work or how to use them
  • Comparing plans like PPO vs. HDHP feels overwhelming without a guided, scenario-based approach
  • Students expressed a desire to "practice" insurance decisions in a low-stakes environment before real life
  • Gamification significantly reduced the perceived difficulty of learning financial and insurance concepts

Survey Results

Gamification Research

Why gamification works for learning

Research shows that gamified learning significantly improves engagement and motivation — making students more likely to stay involved instead of disengaging from complex topics.

🎯

Points & Progress

Core mechanic

Points, levels, and progress bars break down complex topics into achievable steps, reducing cognitive overload and creating a sense of momentum.

⚡

Instant Feedback

Key differentiator

Immediate explanations after each answer — whether correct or incorrect — are more effective than delayed feedback, especially when integrated into a story context.

🏆

Challenges & Rewards

Motivation driver

Achievement badges and Extra Credit challenges motivate continued engagement, making learning feel less overwhelming and more like a game worth completing.

Research Evidence

  • Gamification significantly increases engagement and motivation — students stay involved instead of disengaging from difficult material (Johnson et al., 2016)
  • Combining quiz-based and simulation-based mechanics creates a more engaging and less stressful learning experience than either approach alone
  • Challenge-based gamification improves learning outcomes compared to traditional instruction methods (Kaya & Ercag, 2023)
  • Gamification is particularly effective for health education, improving both knowledge retention and attitude toward the subject (Khoshnoodifar et al., 2023)
Competitive Analysis

Identifying the gap in the market

We analyzed four financial literacy platforms — Zogo, Payback, Banzai, and Everfi. While most target K–12 students with general financial topics, none offer a gamified HSA-specific tool designed for college students transitioning into the workforce.

User Persona

Designing for a real student

Our primary persona represents a college student approaching graduation — smart, busy, and completely unprepared for the healthcare decisions that await them after commencement.

Our Solution

SmartBenefits: Learn by doing

A gamified health literacy platform built specifically for college students. Follow Alex — a new grad navigating his first job offer — through three story-driven modules that build real HSA knowledge.

Module 01
📋

The Offer Letter

Follow Alex as he receives his first job offer and must choose between a PPO and an HDHP + HSA plan. Learn the difference through a realistic scenario before facing it in real life.

Healthcare Basics
Module 02
🎓

HSA Fundamentals

Discover the triple tax advantage of HSAs, understand contribution limits, and learn how HSAs differ from FSAs — all in a bite-sized, quiz-driven format that actually sticks.

HSA Basics
Module 03
💰

HSA in Action

Apply your knowledge to real-world decisions. Which medical expenses qualify? How much should Alex contribute? Extra Credit challenges test advanced compound growth math.

Usability Testing

Testing with real students

We tested our prototype with undergraduate students across different majors, evaluating navigation, content clarity, and the overall learning experience.

🧭

Easy Navigation

Students consistently found the module-by-module flow intuitive. The progress bar and dot indicators made it clear where they were and how much was left.

📖

Realistic Scenarios

The job-offer narrative resonated strongly. Students said scenarios felt relevant and grounded — exactly the kind of situation they expected to face after graduation.

🎮

Gamification Works

Points, achievements, and progress unlocks made learning feel motivating rather than overwhelming. Multiple students said it made HSA concepts feel "way less stressful."

"The gamified format made learning about HSAs and health insurance feel much easier and less stressful to understand. The short modules didn't feel overwhelming at all."
— Usability testing participant, UCI undergraduate

Reflection

Limitations & Future Work

Every project has constraints — and opportunities for what comes next.

Current Limitations

  • Focused on short-term feedback rather than long-term learning retention
  • Financial concepts simplified for accessibility — not exhaustive
  • Prototype covers core HSA ideas, not full real-world edge cases
  • Testing limited to UCI undergraduate population

Future Directions

  • Expand usability testing to students from different universities
  • Add personalized learning paths based on individual user progress
  • Develop a native mobile app for easier everyday access
  • Integrate real HSA provider data for live contribution tracking
